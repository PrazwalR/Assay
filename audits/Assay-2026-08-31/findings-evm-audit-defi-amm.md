## [M-1] JIT liquidity + single-swap tick manipulation lets an LP extract inflated fee and toxicity-surcharge from the next swap in the pool
**Severity**: High
**Category**: evm-audit-defi-amm
**Location**: `AssayHook._afterSwap()` / `AssayHook._beforeSwap()` (src/AssayHook.sol:259-270, 276-308), `AssayHook._donateCeilingOverflow()` (src/AssayHook.sol:385-442), `FeeBlend.ceilingOverflowPips()` (src/libraries/FeeBlend.sol:126-147), `Mispricing.signedTicks()` (src/libraries/Mispricing.sol:27-44)
**Description**: The reference-deviation cap (`PoolTwap.withinBound`, `MAX_REFERENCE_DEVIATION_TICKS = 20,000`) does not defend against pool-tick manipulation at all. It only gates whether a *newly read Chainlink price* is adopted into `state.referenceTick`, by comparing that new reading against `PoolTwap`, a slow (λ=0.99, ~100-block half-life per the deployed `.env`) EWMA of the pool's own historical tick. It never rejects, clamps, or inspects the *live* `poolTick` that the fee and surcharge formulas actually use. Both the LP fee (`quote()`) and the surcharge (`ceilingOverflowPips()`) are driven by `Mispricing.signedTicks(state.referenceTick, state.lastTick, zeroForOne)`, and `state.lastTick` is not read live at quote time — it is whatever the *immediately preceding swap* left in storage. Nothing bounds how far `state.lastTick` may sit from `state.referenceTick` when a swap is quoted except `Mispricing.MAX_MISPRICING_TICKS = 200,000` — ten times larger than the 20,000-tick cap the project's own docs describe as "the" anti-manipulation mechanism. The surcharge is routed via `PoolManager.donate()` to whichever liquidity is currently in-range, and the hook implements no `beforeAddLiquidity`/`afterRemoveLiquidity` restriction, so a JIT liquidity provider can dominate in-range liquidity for exactly the block(s) needed to harvest a surcharge their own manipulation manufactured out of a third party's trade — a scenario `SECURITY.md`'s "Self-dealing on the surcharge" analysis does not cover (that analysis only reasons about an attacker taxing their *own* swap). The live Base Sepolia pool holds only ~$10-20, making both the price-moving trade and out-liquiditying existing LPs trivially cheap.
**Proof of Concept**: (1) Pool sits near equilibrium, quoting `baseFeePips`=500. Attacker observes/anticipates a large pending swap `V`. (2) Attacker adds a large narrow liquidity position straddling the current tick. (3) Attacker submits swap `A`, walking `state.lastTick` so `V`'s direction will classify as "capturing drift." A ~5,000-tick displacement (≈65% price move — well under the 200,000-tick `Mispricing` clamp and even under the 20,000-tick deviation cap, so `ReferenceDeviationCapTripped` never fires) already produces, with the deployed `captureShareBps=1000`: raw = 500 + 5000·10 = 50,500 pips, `ceilingOverflowPips` = 50,500 − 10,000 = 40,500/1,000,000 ≈ 4.05% of `V`'s notional as *additional* donate, on top of the 1% (`maxFeePips`) LP fee. (4) `V` executes next and pays ~1% LP fee + ~4% donate, credited via `donate()` to in-range liquidity dominated by the attacker. (5) Attacker removes liquidity, collecting the accrued fee growth, netting most of ~5% of `V`'s notional minus setup cost. No invariant in `test/invariant/AssayInvariants.t.sol` checks for this value-transfer property.
**Recommendation**: Derive the fee/surcharge's tick input from a value that itself costs sustained, cross-block capital to move (e.g., `PoolTwap`'s own smoothed anchor) rather than from `state.lastTick` set by the immediately preceding swap. Separately, cap the donate surcharge at a small fixed ceiling independent of `MAX_OVERFLOW_PIPS`, and/or introduce a minimum liquidity-residency window so a position added and removed within the same block as the swap it profits from does not receive a full pro-rata share of that swap's surcharge.

## [M-2] `feeBounds()` (and `SECURITY.md`'s advertised fee-bound guarantee) omit the toxicity-surcharge donation, understating the true worst-case cost of a swap
**Severity**: Medium
**Category**: evm-audit-defi-amm
**Location**: `AssayHook.feeBounds()` (src/AssayHook.sol:102-110), `AssayHook._donateCeilingOverflow()` (src/AssayHook.sol:385-442), `FeeBlend.ceilingOverflowPips()` (src/libraries/FeeBlend.sol:104-147), `ToxicitySurcharge.surchargeAmount()` (src/libraries/ToxicitySurcharge.sol:53-64)
**Description**: `feeBounds()` is documented as existing so "a router or LP needs to know the worst fee a swap can be quoted before routing into the pool," returning `(baseFeePips, minFeePips, maxFeePips)`. `SECURITY.md` repeats this as a held property ("Every quoted fee lies within `[minFeePips, maxFeePips]`"), enforced by `invariant_QuotedFeeAlwaysWithinAdvertisedBounds`. That property covers only the LP fee from `_quote()`. It says nothing about the separate donate surcharge computed in `_afterSwap`, which is deducted from the swapper's own settlement on top of the already-charged `maxFeePips`, bounded only by `FeeBlend.MAX_OVERFLOW_PIPS = 1,000,000` — up to 100% of the swap's unspecified-side notional in an extreme dislocation. Any integrator sizing slippage tolerance from `feeBounds().maxFeePips` alone is exposed to this undisclosed additional deduction. Combined with M-1, this is reachable with a moderate, easily-triggered drift that never trips the hook's own deviation-cap circuit breaker.
**Proof of Concept**: (1) Integrator reads `feeBounds()`, sees `maxFeePips=10,000` (1%), sizes `amountOutMinimum` assuming ~1% max fee. (2) A swap executes while `quotedDrift` is large (organically, or via M-1's manipulation), and `_donateCeilingOverflow` donates an additional ~4-9%+ of notional not reflected in `feeBounds()` or the swap's LP-fee event. (3) Swapper receives materially less than `feeBounds()` implied.
**Recommendation**: Expose the surcharge's worst case alongside `feeBounds()` (e.g., a `maxSurchargePips()` getter, or fold `MAX_OVERFLOW_PIPS` into a combined worst-case value). Update `SECURITY.md`'s stated property to explicitly scope it to the LP fee and separately document the surcharge's own, much larger bound.
